📚 Economics explained, in plain words

What is money? Something everyone agrees to swap for stuff. It helps us trade without bartering chickens for shoes.

What is inflation? When prices go up over time, so the same money buys less. A little is normal; a lot hurts savings.

Why are currencies different? Each country runs its own money. How strong it is depends on what the country makes and sells, how much money is printed, interest rates and how much people trust it.

Gross domestic product is the value of everything a country makes in a year. Per person divides that by the number of people, so big and small countries can be compared fairly.

🏦 Having a real wallet, in plain words

There are two main ways to own real coins. Neither is perfect. Not advice, just the good and bad sides.

📱 An app or exchange holds them for you

Like Revolut, Coinbase, Kraken or Binance. The company keeps the secret keys.

Good sides
  • Very easy, feels like a bank app
  • Buy with normal money from your bank card
  • Forgot your password? Support can help
  • Usually regulated in your country
Bad sides
  • "Not your keys, not your coins": the company holds them
  • If it goes bust or gets hacked, coins can be lost
  • It can freeze your account or limit withdrawals
  • Fees and price spreads can be high
  • Some apps won't let you move coins out

🔑 You hold them yourself

A software wallet on your phone, or a hardware wallet (a small device that keeps keys offline).

Good sides
  • You hold the keys, no one can freeze them
  • Works with any app or exchange
  • Hardware wallets keep keys away from the internet
  • No company in the middle
Bad sides
  • Lose your recovery words and the coins are gone forever
  • No support desk to call
  • Fake wallet apps and scams target beginners
  • Usually you still need an exchange to buy with normal money
  • Hardware wallets cost money

Golden rule: nobody real will ever ask for your recovery words. Not support, not a friend, not us.
